ONE SMALL START

Choose a message worth sharing

Start with an interest or an ordinary choice. Ask what the child would like you to understand. Choose one useful message and agree what you will do when you notice it.

“Sometimes I might get the wrong idea. Can we choose a quick way for you to let me know?”

Speaking, pointing, writing, drawing or showing a card all count. They can change the words or decide not to use a tool.

A quick guide for the EA

Before a difficult moment

Approach privately where possible. Use one clear request and allow time. Offer genuine choices about how to do the task. Keep the agreed communication option within reach.

If talking gets harder

Reduce talking and the audience. Try: “We can pause. You do not need to explain right now.” Offer support even if the child cannot use their signal.

When you notice the message

PRIVATE: offer the agreed private place.
CHECK: pause your assumption and check.
PAUSE: follow the agreed pause plan.
WRITE: accept writing without requiring speech too.

When they are ready later

Ask, “Was there something you wanted me to know?” Accept “not now”. Revisit any practical task privately with a manageable next step.

Help does not depend on eye contact, a full sentence, an apology or a particular tone. If a request cannot be met exactly, explain briefly and offer a workable option. Follow the school’s usual supervision and safety arrangements.

Ideas to choose from

Correct a harmless mix-up

Agree that you are acting: “Let’s pretend I have misunderstood your design idea.” The child can use CHECK or their own words. Acknowledge the correction and follow it.

Choose a different way

During a suitable activity: “Would you like to write the idea, sketch it or tell me?” Follow the choice where the task allows.

Practise asking for privacy

In a comfortable private moment, try the chosen signal and your agreed response. Do not stage a public confrontation.

Draw it as a story

Three panels: what happened, my message, the listener’s response. Let the child choose the characters and style.

A brief, agreed practice is enough. Model once if useful, accept a decision to pass, and never create distress to test the skill.
